# LexCrosscheck
**No Unchecked Citations. No Hidden Conflicts.**
- HTML Mirror:  [https://roxanneardary.com/lexcrosscheck/](https://roxanneardary.com/lexcrosscheck/)  

---

LexCrosscheck is a modular retrieval-augmented generation specification for legal research, legal research synthesis, citation verification, jurisdictional analysis, case briefs, legal writing, and legal arguments. It is designed to retrieve authoritative legal sources, verify citations and source links, identify conflicts between jurisdictions and authorities, assess research risks, and keep humans in control of consequential legal decisions.

## Purpose

LexCrosscheck provides an architecture for building source-grounded legal research systems that can understand natural language legal questions and identify the laws, regulations, court decisions, rules, and other authorities relevant to a matter.

LexCrosscheck must distinguish retrieved legal authority from AI-generated analysis and human-selected legal positions. It must not present unsupported legal conclusions as authoritative law.

## Core Principles

- Source-grounded legal research
- Mandatory citation for substantive legal claims
- Direct source links whenever publicly available
- Citation and source verification before presentation
- Jurisdiction-aware retrieval and analysis
- Modular legal-domain and jurisdiction architecture
- Detection and disclosure of conflicting authorities
- Temporal awareness of legal authority
- Counterargument and adverse-authority research
- Visible risk and uncertainty warnings
- Human-in-the-loop decision making
- Complete research auditability
- No fabricated authorities, citations, quotations, or URLs
- Vendor-neutral and model-neutral architecture
- Extensible module and plugin system
- Separation of legal authority, AI analysis, and human judgment

## Natural Language Legal Intake Module

The Natural Language Legal Intake Module must accept legal questions, factual descriptions, documents, and other natural language inputs.

The module must identify and structure:

- Legal questions
- Potential legal issues
- Relevant facts
- Parties and entities
- Locations
- Dates and relevant time periods
- Transactions and events
- Property
- Courts and government agencies
- Claims and defenses
- Rights and obligations
- Potential remedies
- Relevant areas of law
- Potentially applicable jurisdictions
- Missing or disputed facts
- User-provided facts
- Retrieved facts
- AI inferences
- Assumptions
- Unknown information

The module must clearly distinguish facts supplied by the user from facts retrieved from legal sources and conclusions generated by the system.

## Jurisdiction Identification Module

The Jurisdiction Identification Module must determine which jurisdictions may be relevant to a legal matter.

It must consider:

- Federal jurisdiction
- State jurisdiction
- County jurisdiction
- Municipal jurisdiction
- Township jurisdiction
- City jurisdiction
- Local administrative jurisdiction
- Tribal jurisdiction
- Territorial jurisdiction
- Trial courts
- Appellate courts
- Specialized courts
- Administrative bodies

The module should consider physical location, residence, property location, place of transaction, place of conduct, choice-of-law provisions, forum-selection provisions, subject-matter jurisdiction, personal jurisdiction, procedural posture, and other jurisdictional factors.

The module must identify potentially relevant jurisdictions before conducting jurisdiction-sensitive legal research.

## Source Authority Module

The Source Authority Module must classify retrieved materials according to their legal authority.

It must distinguish between:

- Primary authority
- Secondary authority
- Binding authority
- Persuasive authority
- Nonbinding authority
- Government guidance
- Commentary
- User-provided materials

Primary authority must be preferred when primary authority is required. A secondary source must never be silently substituted for the underlying primary authority.

## Source Verification Module

The Source Verification Module must verify each substantive legal source before the system presents it as verified.

Verification should include:

- Source existence
- Source identity
- Citation accuracy
- Jurisdiction
- Court or issuing authority
- Authority type
- Publication information
- Effective date
- Repeal status
- Amendment status
- Precedential status
- Current validity
- Source URL
- Direct accessibility

The system must attempt to access a cited source before labeling its link as verified.

If verification cannot be completed, the system must clearly identify the source as unverified or inaccessible.

The system must never fabricate a source or URL.

## Citation Validation Module

The Citation Validation Module must verify that citations are accurate and correspond to the legal authority they are intended to support.

Validation must consider:

- Citation format
- Case name
- Court
- Date
- Reporter information
- Docket number
- Statute
- Regulation
- Section
- Subsection
- Source URL
- Jurisdiction
- Authority type

The module must flag or reject citations that cannot be verified.

## Direct-Link Verification Module

Every substantive legal authority cited by LexCrosscheck must include a direct public source link whenever a public source is available.

The system must test the destination before identifying the link as verified.

Links must be categorized according to verification status, including:

- Verified direct source
- Verified landing page
- Redirected source
- Inaccessible source
- Unverified source
- Broken source

Official government and court sources should be preferred.

The system must never invent a replacement URL when the original source cannot be located.

## Citation Integrity Module

The Citation Integrity Module must evaluate the relationship between legal claims and their supporting authorities.

It must identify:

- Unsupported claims
- Incorrect citations
- Authority mismatches
- Overbroad interpretations
- Misquoted authority
- Out-of-context authority
- Superseded authority
- Overruled authority
- Repealed law
- Wrong jurisdiction
- Wrong court
- Wrong date
- Wrong statutory section
- Incorrect procedural history

Every material legal claim should be traceable to the authority supporting it.

## Evidence-to-Claim Mapping Module

LexCrosscheck must maintain an evidence chain connecting legal conclusions to their supporting sources.

The minimum relationship must be:

**Claim → Authority → Exact Source Location → Verification Status**

The system should preserve the relevant quotation, passage, section, paragraph, page, or other source location when available.

## Jurisdiction Conflict Detection Module

The Jurisdiction Conflict Detection Module must identify conflicts between legal authorities from different jurisdictions or levels of government.

Potential conflicts include differences involving:

- Statutory requirements
- Definitions
- Deadlines
- Procedures
- Legal standards
- Elements of claims
- Defenses
- Remedies
- Damages
- Regulatory requirements
- Privacy obligations
- Tax obligations
- Contract requirements
- Precedent

When a conflict is detected, the system must identify:

- The jurisdictions involved
- The competing authorities
- The nature of the conflict
- Authority level
- Geographic scope
- Temporal scope
- Potential controlling authority
- Potential resolution mechanisms
- Remaining uncertainty

Material conflicts must not be silently resolved by the AI.

## Federal-State Conflict Module

The Federal-State Conflict Module must analyze potential conflicts between federal and state authority.

It should evaluate:

- Express preemption
- Implied preemption
- Conflict preemption
- Field preemption
- Supremacy Clause considerations
- Savings provisions
- Concurrent authority
- Federalism considerations

The module must distinguish between an actual legal conflict and authorities that merely appear different because they regulate different issues.

## Conflict Resolution Module

The Conflict Resolution Module must organize competing authorities according to relevant legal principles.

Potential considerations include:

- Authority hierarchy
- Jurisdiction
- Subject matter
- Geographic applicability
- Temporal applicability
- Preemption
- Precedent
- Statutory construction
- Regulatory authority
- Choice-of-law provisions
- Forum-selection provisions

The module must present competing interpretations and identify the factors that may determine which authority controls.

A material conflict must produce a human decision gate rather than an automatically imposed legal position.

## Temporal Law Module

The Temporal Law Module must determine which version of a legal authority applies to the relevant date.

It must track:

- Enactment date
- Effective date
- Amendment date
- Repeal date
- Sunset provisions
- Historical versions
- Prospective application
- Retroactive application
- Transitional provisions

The system must not apply current law automatically when historical law is required.

- Vacatur

- Adoption
- Affirmance
- Subsequent interpretation

A search result or citation relationship must not automatically be treated as evidence that a case remains controlling.

- Low
- Moderate

- Unverified authority

- Ambiguous law
- Material adverse authority
- Weak source support
- Significant procedural uncertainty

## Uncertainty Module

LexCrosscheck must explicitly represent uncertainty.

Uncertainty may arise from:

- Missing facts
- Ambiguous language
- Conflicting authority
- Incomplete source access
- Uncertain precedent
- Historical law
- Jurisdictional ambiguity
- Incomplete retrieval
- Conflicting interpretations
- Insufficient authority

The system must not convert uncertainty into certainty merely to produce a definitive response.

## Retrieval Confidence and Legal Certainty

LexCrosscheck must keep retrieval confidence separate from legal certainty.

A highly relevant search result does not establish that a legal proposition is correct.

The system must distinguish:

**Retrieval Confidence:** Confidence that the retrieved source is relevant to the research question.

**Legal Certainty:** Confidence that the legal proposition is supported by applicable authority after considering jurisdiction, authority hierarchy, temporal applicability, precedent, conflicting authorities, and available facts.

## Human-in-the-Loop Governance Module

LexCrosscheck must maintain human control over consequential legal decisions.

The AI must not:

- Decide which conflicting jurisdiction controls without human authorization
- Represent itself as a lawyer
- Make irreversible legal decisions on behalf of a user
- Override human judgment
- Suppress adverse authority
- Remove material risk warnings
- Mark an unverified citation as verified
- Convert uncertainty into certainty
- Present an AI interpretation as binding law

Human reviewers must be able to:

- Accept or reject authorities
- Add sources
- Reject interpretations
- Modify factual assumptions
- Select or reject jurisdictions
- Approve or reject arguments
- Request additional research
- Resolve identified conflicts
- Approve final documentation

Material human decisions should be recorded in the audit trail.

## Human Decision Gate

When LexCrosscheck identifies a material legal conflict, significant uncertainty, or consequential decision, it must provide a Human Decision Gate.

The gate should present:

- Legal issue
- Relevant jurisdictions
- Competing authorities
- Conflict description
- AI analysis
- Risk level
- Uncertainty
- Missing information
- Recommended additional research
- Available decision options

The system must pause or clearly defer the consequential decision until the human reviewer determines how to proceed.

## Source Priority Module

LexCrosscheck should prioritize sources in the following order:

- Official court sources
- Official legislative sources
- Official government sources
- Official regulatory or administrative sources
- Verified public legal repositories
- Secondary legal databases
- Legal commentary

Higher-priority sources should be preferred when multiple sources contain substantially equivalent authority.

## Source Failure Handling Module

When a source cannot be accessed or verified, LexCrosscheck must:

- Report the failure
- Preserve the original citation
- Identify the verification status
- Attempt an authoritative alternative source when appropriate
- Distinguish the alternative source from the original source
- Never fabricate a replacement citation or URL

## Plugin Architecture

LexCrosscheck must support optional plugins that extend functionality without making those capabilities mandatory to the core specification.

Plugins may provide:

- Additional legal jurisdictions
- Additional legal domains
- Specialized court databases
- Additional source repositories
- Legal citation formats
- Document formats
- Source monitoring
- Docket monitoring
- Legislative tracking
- Regulatory tracking
- Case-law treatment analysis
- Advanced conflict analysis
- Specialized legal research workflows
- Translation
- Document comparison
- Legal document ingestion
- External verification services
- Human review workflows
- Organization-specific research policies

Optional plugins must identify their capabilities, supported jurisdictions, supported authority types, source dependencies, verification capabilities, version, and limitations.

Plugins must not bypass core citation, verification, conflict disclosure, risk, or human-governance requirements.

## Plugin Verification Requirements

Optional plugins that provide legal sources or legal analysis must identify the origin and authority level of their data.

Plugins must not cause unverified material to be represented as verified.

Plugin-generated legal content remains subject to the same citation, source verification, conflict detection, risk assessment, and human review requirements as core system output.

## Example Research Flow

A typical LexCrosscheck workflow should:

- Receive a natural language legal question
- Identify relevant facts and missing information
- Identify potentially applicable jurisdictions
- Identify relevant legal domains
- Activate appropriate jurisdiction and legal-domain modules
- Generate a structured research plan
- Retrieve primary and secondary authorities
- Verify source identity and accessibility
- Validate citations
- Determine temporal applicability
- Evaluate precedential treatment
- Search for adverse authorities and counterarguments
- Detect jurisdictional conflicts
- Assess risk and uncertainty
- Generate source-grounded analysis
- Present citations and tested source links
- Present conflicts and risks
- Request human decisions where required
- Generate the requested legal documentation
- Preserve the research audit trail

## Example Conflict Output

When authorities conflict, the system should identify:

**Issue:** The legal requirement under review.

**Jurisdiction A:** Applicable authority and source.

**Jurisdiction B:** Conflicting authority and source.

**Conflict:** Specific difference between the authorities.

**Authority Level:** Relative authority of each source.

**Temporal Scope:** Relevant effective dates and historical versions.

**Geographic Scope:** Where each authority applies.

**Potential Resolution:** Applicable principles such as preemption, hierarchy, precedent, or choice of law.

**Risk:** Current risk level and reasons.

**Human Decision:** The decision that requires human review.
